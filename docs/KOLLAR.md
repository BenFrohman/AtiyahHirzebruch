This is the second classical counterexample to *integral* Hodge. It is not Clay Term B.

## Theorem (Kollár)

Let \(X\subset\mathbb{P}^4\) be a very general hypersurface of degree \(D=125\). Lefschetz plus duality give

\[
H^2(X,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\,h,
\qquad
H^4(X,\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}\,\alpha
\quad\text{with}\quad
\int_X \alpha\cup h=1.
\]

Then \(125\alpha=h^3\) in cohomology, so \(\alpha\) is an integral Hodge class of infinite order. Every curve on \(X\) has degree divisible by \(5\). Therefore \(\alpha\) is not algebraic.

Reference: J. Kollár, in *Classification of irregular varieties* (Trento, 1990), LNM 1515, Springer, 1992, p. 134.

## Why the rational conjecture survives

After \(\otimes\mathbb{Q}\) one has \(\alpha=h^3/125\), which is a rational multiple of an algebraic class. The miss is integral. The Clay map is rational.
