\begin{abstract}
Standalone formal documentation and mathematical ledger of the 1962 Atiyah-Hirzebruch torsion counterexamples to the Integral Hodge Conjecture on smooth projective varieties over C, constructed via Steenrod operations in topological K-theory on Godeaux-Serre quotients of products of elliptic curves by elementary abelian p-groups (p >= 3) in complex dimension >= 7. Explicitly documents why torsion classes vanish under tensoring with Q (H\textasciicircum{}\{2p\}(X, Z)\_tors (x) Q = 0) and therefore do not constitute counterexamples to the Clay Mathematics Institute Rational Hodge Conjecture.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope, Scientific Honesty, and Non-Claim Boundary}
This repository records known 1962 integral torsion obstructions (Atiyah-Hirzebruch) and their vanishing after rationalization. It does not prove or disprove the rational Clay Hodge Conjecture.

This shelf records one of the two classical counterexamples to

```

CH\textasciicircum{}k(X) → H\textasciicircum{}\{2k\}(X,Z) ∩ H\textasciicircum{}\{k,k\}(X)

```

being surjective. The other shelf is Kollár (non-torsion). Neither shelf is Term B of rational Hodge.

There exist smooth complex projective varieties and torsion classes

```

α ∈ H\textasciicircum{}\{2k\}(X,Z) ∩ H\textasciicircum{}\{k,k\}(X),    nα = 0 for some n > 0,

```

that are not the class of an algebraic cycle.

Source: M. F. Atiyah and F. Hirzebruch, *Analytic cycles on complex manifolds*, Topology 1 (1962), 25–45.

Totaro (1997) recast the same examples in complex cobordism: the refined cycle class `CH\textasciicircum{}*(X) → MU\textasciicircum{}*(X) ⊗\_\{MU\_*\} Z` misses those torsion classes.
